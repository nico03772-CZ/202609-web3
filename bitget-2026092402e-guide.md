## 2. What Is Copy Trading?

Copy trading means you authorize the system to replicate another trader's orders. When they open or close a position, your account executes the same action based on the capital ratio and limits you set in advance.

On Bitget, copy trading generally splits into two types:

- **Futures copy trading:**&#8203; mirrors leveraged derivative positions. PnL swings are larger, and it suits people who already understand liquidation.
- **Spot copy trading:**&#8203; mirrors spot buys and sells with no leverage. Swings are milder, which makes it easier for beginners.

Beyond the product difference, one thing must be clear: **you are not handing money to the trader**. Funds stay in your account; the trader only shares trade signals.

The most common mistake is sorting by ROI and picking the top name. That number usually comes from high leverage plus a short lucky streak. Look at these instead:

- **Returns across periods:**&#8203; check three-month, six-month, and one-year performance rather than just seven days.
- **Maximum drawdown:**&#8203; the deepest loss ever experienced. A large drawdown means you must be able to handle serious psychological pressure.
- **Number of copiers and total copied capital:**&#8203; reflects trust, but is not a safety guarantee.
- **Win rate and total trades:**&#8203; a high win rate with very few trades is not enough data to judge.
- **Trade frequency:**&#8203; too frequent inflates fee costs; too infrequent leaves capital idle.
- **Account lifespan:**&#8203; an account that has survived multiple market phases is more credible than one that trended for a few weeks.
- **Leverage used:**&#8203; high leverage makes PnL swing violently. Beginners should avoid it.

A practical approach: pick three to five traders with different styles, watch them for one to two weeks with small capital, then scale up on the one that fits best.
