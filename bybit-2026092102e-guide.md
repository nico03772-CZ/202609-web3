## What is a Bybit contract: understand USDT-settled contracts

Bybit contracts mainly come in two types: USDT-settled contracts (margin in a stablecoin like USDT) and coin-settled contracts (margin in BTC, ETH, etc.). Beginners most often use **USDT-settled contracts**, such as BTCUSDT and ETHUSDT, because both margin and profit/loss are denominated in USDT, which is intuitive.

Core traits of contract trading:

- **Leverage**: Amplifies your capital efficiency, e.g. 10x, 20x, 100x. Higher leverage means less margin required, but the profit/loss swing from price moves is also larger.
- **Long / Short**: Bullish = "Buy/Long"; bearish = "Sell/Short".
- **Liquidation price**: When losses hit your margin level, the position is force-closed.

2. Pick a trading pair, e.g. **BTCUSDT**.
3. Choose a margin mode:

## How to set take-profit and stop-loss (TP / SL)

Take-profit (TP) and stop-loss (SL) are the most basic risk-control tools in contract trading. Bybit supports two ways to set them.

### Method 1: Set them together when opening

Below the order ticket, find the "Take Profit / Stop Loss" section:

- Enable "Take Profit" and enter the **trigger price** (price that activates it) and the **order price** (the actual limit price placed, usually close to the trigger).
- Enable "Stop Loss" and enter the trigger price and order price.
- You can also enter a "profit/loss amount" or "percentage" directly, and Bybit converts it to a price.

Once the order fills, TP/SL is placed automatically — no need to watch the screen.

### Method 2: Set after you already hold the position

If you forgot at open, you can still add it later:

1. In the "Positions" list, find that position.
2. Click "TP/SL" or the pencil icon.
3. Add TP / SL conditions, setting the trigger price and order price.
4. Confirm, and the conditional order appears under the position.

### Trigger price vs. order price

- **Trigger Price**: When market price reaches this level, the system "activates" your TP/SL order.
- **Order Price**: The actual price placed after activation. If left blank, it defaults to market or latest price.

Example: You go long BTCUSDT at 60,000 and want to take profit at 65,000 and stop out at 57,000. Set TP trigger at 65,000 and SL trigger at 57,000; the order price can match the trigger or be slightly wider to avoid slippage in extreme moves.

### Trailing Stop

Bybit also offers a "Trailing Stop": as price moves in your favor, the stop level rises (long) or falls (short), locking in unrealized profit — ideal for trending markets.
