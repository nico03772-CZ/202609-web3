## 1. The Core Difference: Market Order vs. Limit Order

### Market Order
Buys or sells immediately at the **best available price** on the order book.
- **Trait**: guaranteed fill, no guarantee on price.
- **Slippage**: in thin liquidity or with large size, the fill can differ noticeably from the quoted price.
- **Fees**: typically the higher **taker** rate — your order "takes" liquidity from resting orders.

### Limit Order
You set a price; the order fills only when the market reaches it (or better).
- **Trait**: guaranteed price, no guaranteed fill — it rests until touched.
- **Slippage**: essentially none; you fill at your price or better.
- **Fees**: filled limit orders usually earn the lower **maker** rate, since your order adds liquidity to the book.

### Quick Comparison

| Factor | Market Order | Limit Order |
|:---|:---|:---|
| Fill speed | Instant | Waits for the price |
| Price control | None (slippage possible) | Exact to your level |
| Fee tier | Taker (higher) | Maker (lower) |
| Best for | Chasing moves, stop-outs | Planned entries, cost control |
| Main risk | Slippage raises cost | May never fill, missed move |

**One-liner**: market orders buy speed, limit orders buy price. For frequent contract traders, the maker/taker gap compounds into serious money over time.

## 3. Opening a Contract Position With a Limit Order (8 Steps)

Example: going long on the BTCUSDT perpetual (shorting works the same, just inverted).

**Step 1: Open the derivatives terminal**
In the Bybit app or web → Trade → Derivatives, select your contract (e.g., BTCUSDT perpetual).

**Step 2: Check account and margin mode**
Cross margin shares all available balance as collateral across positions — harder to liquidate but bigger exposure. Isolated margin confines collateral to each position — a clearer loss cap. Beginners should start isolated.

**Step 3: Set leverage**
Start at 2x–5x. Higher leverage moves the liquidation price closer and shrinks your margin for error.

**Step 4: Switch the order type to "Limit"**&#8203;
In the order panel, change the default "Market" to "Limit."

**Step 5: Enter price and size**
- **Price**: anchor to a support level or your planned entry; for longs, set below spot and wait for the pullback.
- **Size**: work from position value — make sure notional ÷ margin stays within your planned leverage.
- Set **take-profit and stop-loss (TP/SL)**&#8203; in the same panel so they arm on fill.

**Step 6: Review and submit**
Double-check direction, price, size, and leverage, then submit. The order appears under "Open Orders."

**Step 7: Monitor and adjust**
Until the price touches your level, the order simply rests. Cancel or amend it anytime from Open Orders. Once filled, the position shows under "Positions."

**Step 8: Never leave a position without TP/SL**
If you skipped TP/SL at entry, add it immediately after the fill. A leveraged position without a stop-loss hands risk management to luck.

## 5. Common Beginner Mistakes

1. **Chasing candles with market orders**: in volatile moments the fill can be far off — even for urgent orders, glance at the book depth first.
2. **Placing limits too far away**: an untouchable price means the move finishes without you.
3. **No stop-loss on contract positions**: with leverage and liquidation, the worst case is liquidation, not just being stuck holding.
4. **Cross margin + high leverage**: stacked risk — one wick can wipe out a large chunk of equity.
5. **Confusing limit orders with conditional orders**: a limit order rests and reserves margin logic immediately; a conditional order only activates after a trigger. Different tools.
