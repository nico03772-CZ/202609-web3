## 3. Quick Refresher: What Is a Perpetual Contract?

A perpetual contract is a derivative with no expiry date. You can go long or short without any physical delivery. To keep the contract price from drifting away from the spot price, the exchange uses a **funding fee**: at set intervals, whichever side is dominant pays the other side.

The word that follows, USDT or inverse, is what determines how margin and PnL are calculated.

## 4. What Is a USDT Perpetual Contract (USDT-M)?

This is the most common type on Bybit. The core feature: **both margin and settlement are in USDT**, a dollar-pegged stablecoin.

- Open a BTC long on USDT-M, and your PnL is denominated in USDT.
- The math is intuitive: one dollar of profit is one USDT.
- A single USDT margin account can trade BTC, ETH, SOL, and other pairs without depositing each coin separately.
- Liquidity is usually the deepest and spreads the tightest, which suits beginners.

In short: USDT-M is a contract traded with a stablecoin, and everything settles in USDT.

## 5. What Is an Inverse Contract (Coin-M)?

An inverse contract is quoted in USD but **margined and settled in the base coin itself**. On a BTC inverse contract, both margin and PnL are calculated in BTC.

A concrete example: you hold 0.5 BTC and go long on the BTC inverse contract. If BTC rises, your profit is credited in BTC. If BTC falls, the BTC in your account is losing value at the same time, and the loss on the contract is also denominated in BTC.

Why is it called inverse? Because the contract structure is the reverse of a linear one: for an equal move in the underlying price, the position value measured in coin does not change linearly, it moves along the reciprocal of price. Because of this non-linearity, the further price travels in one direction, the more sharply PnL swings when measured in coin.

## 7. Which One Should You Choose?

**Choose USDT-M if:**&#8203;
- You are a beginner and want simple math and manageable risk;
- Your capital is in stablecoins and you do not hold BTC or ETH yet;
- You trade multiple pairs and want one shared margin account.

**Choose an inverse Coin-M contract if:**&#8203;
- You already hold spot coins long term and want to use that coin as margin;
- Your goal is to **grow your coin count**: profits settle in coin, so a winning long accumulates more BTC or ETH without selling your holdings;
- You want to hedge the downside risk of coins you already hold.

A practical tip: long-term holders often use inverse contracts to keep the coin and run a position at the same time. If your capital is in USDT, USDT-M is almost always the more convenient route.
